What Is Gas Furnace Installation and When Is It Needed?

Gas furnace installation means fitting a new gas furnace in your home and connecting it to gas, power, venting and ductwork. Then it's tested until it runs safely. It sounds simple, but the furnace has to suit the house, and every connection has to be right.

Ottawa winters stay below freezing for months, so a furnace that quits is a real problem. Ottawa Gas Furnace helps homeowners and businesses pick the right unit and put it in properly.

What the job involves

A proper job has more steps than most people expect.

  • Sizing: We estimate your home's heat loss so the furnace isn't too big or too small.
  • Removal: The old unit is disconnected, the gas is capped, and the furnace is carried out.
  • Setting and connecting: The new furnace is leveled and joined to the ducts, gas line, vent and electrical supply.
  • Drain and thermostat: A high efficiency model needs a condensate drain, and the thermostat gets wired in.
  • Testing: We check gas pressure, the flame, the exhaust and airflow before we call it done.

When you need a new furnace

Most people don't plan for this. The furnace picks its own moment, usually in January. A few situations point to it clearly.

  • Your furnace is 15 to 20 years old and the repair bills keep climbing.
  • The heat exchanger is cracked. That can't be fixed safely, so the unit has to go.
  • You're finishing a basement, adding a room or renovating, and the old furnace can't carry the load.
  • You're switching from oil or electric heat to gas.
  • You want lower gas bills and a high efficiency model makes sense.

Gas Furnace Installation

This is the full job for a home that needs a furnace put in, whether it's a new build, a renovation or a switch from oil or electric heat. We size the unit, set it, connect gas, venting and power, and test it before we leave.

Furnace Replacement

Most calls start here. The old furnace is 15 to 20 years old, loud, or costing more each winter. We pull it out, fit the new one to your existing ducts and thermostat where we can, and check that the vent still suits the new unit.

High-Efficiency Furnace Installation

A condensing furnace pulls more heat from the same gas, so it can cut your bill. It vents through plastic pipe and needs a drain for the water it makes. We check where that pipe and drain can run before we quote.

Two-Stage Furnace Installation

A two stage furnace runs on low most of the time and on high only when it's very cold. You get steadier room temperatures and quieter running. It suits larger homes and anyone tired of cold spots upstairs.

Furnace Removal and Disposal

The old furnace has to come out cleanly. We shut off and cap the gas, disconnect the venting, carry the unit out and haul it away. Old units are heavy, and tight basement stairs make this harder than it looks.

Gas Line Connections

A new furnace needs a gas supply that's the right size and tightly sealed. We connect the line, add a shutoff and a drip leg where needed, and test every joint for leaks. Gas work is regulated in Ontario for good reason.

Venting and Flue Work

Exhaust gases must leave the house, not drift back in. We check the vent pipe for correct slope, size and sealing, and replace what doesn't fit the new furnace. An old chimney often needs attention when the furnace is upgraded.

Thermostat Installation

A new furnace works better with a thermostat that matches it. We install and set up a programmable or smart model, and make sure two stage and high efficiency units get the right wiring. You get a quick walkthrough on using it.

Ductwork Adjustments

Sometimes the furnace is fine and the ducts are the problem. We modify the plenum and main trunk so the new unit connects properly, seal leaks we can reach, and fix a return that's too small. Small changes here can fix hot and cold rooms.

Heat Load Calculations

Bigger isn't better. An oversized furnace cycles on and off and wears out sooner. We work out how much heat your house loses based on size, insulation, windows and layout, then pick a furnace output that fits.

Humidifier Installation

Winter air here gets very dry, which means static shocks, cracked skin and sore throats. We mount a humidifier on the furnace so it adds moisture while the system runs. It's easiest to add at the same time as the new furnace.

Combustion Safety Testing

After the install we test how the furnace burns. We check gas pressure, the flame, the exhaust and carbon monoxide levels at the vent and in the room. It takes a short time, and it's the check that matters most.

Signs You Need Professional Gas Furnace Installation

Furnaces rarely quit without warning. Most drop hints for a season or two first, and an Ottawa winter makes those hints expensive to ignore. Here's what owners notice, and what each sign usually means before you start thinking about gas furnace installation.

  • Rising energy bills

    Your gas bill creeps up, but your thermostat setting and habits haven't changed. That often points to a tired furnace burning more fuel for the same heat. Many furnaces from the 1990s run at 80 percent efficiency or less. New high efficiency models reach 95 percent or more.

  • Uneven heating

    The living room is warm and the back bedroom stays cold. Sometimes the ducts are the problem. Other times the furnace is too small, or its blower is worn and can't push air far enough. We check both before suggesting anything.

  • Banging or squealing noises

    A loud bang when the burners light can mean delayed ignition. Squealing usually points to a worn blower motor or bearing. Scraping means something is rubbing inside the housing. A new noise now and then is normal. A noise that keeps coming back is not.

  • Frequent repair calls

    One repair is part of owning a furnace. Two or three in a single winter is a pattern. If you're paying for another part on a unit past its mid teens, I'd put that money toward a new furnace instead.

  • Yellow flame or gas smell

    A healthy burner flame is steady and blue. Yellow or flickering flames, soot near the furnace, or a rotten egg smell are safety warnings. A cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.

  • A furnace over 15 years old

    Check the label on the cabinet for the build date. Most gas furnaces last 15 to 20 years. Past that, parts get harder to find and efficiency keeps sliding. Replacing it on your schedule beats replacing it in January.

Gas Furnace Installation Solutions for Different Needs

A bungalow in Nepean and a small shop in Kanata don't need the same furnace. Size, layout, venting and budget all change the answer. Here's what we usually recommend for three common situations, and why.

For homeowners

For most family homes, we lean toward a two stage furnace. It runs on low for most of the winter and only kicks up to high on the coldest days. You get steadier temperatures, less noise and fewer hot and cold swings. It costs more than a single stage unit, but you feel the difference daily.

For older properties

Older homes bring surprises. A high efficiency furnace vents through plastic pipe out a side wall, so an old chimney may no longer be the right path. Ducts may be undersized or leaky. We look at all of it first, because a new furnace on bad ductwork won't heat well.

For small businesses

Shops and offices need a furnace sized to the actual space, not a guess by square footage. Open floor plans, high ceilings and doors that open all day change the load. We plan the work around your open hours so you're not heating an empty building or losing a trading day.

Tools, Equipment and Methods Used for Gas Furnace Installation

A furnace is only as good as the setup behind it. The right gear lets a technician measure instead of guess. That's the difference between heat that works on day one and a callback in February.

  • Manual J load calculation

    This method works out how much heat your home actually loses, using insulation, windows, size and layout. It stops us from installing a furnace that's too big or too small. An oversized unit short cycles, wears out sooner and leaves rooms uneven.

  • Combustion analyzer

    This handheld meter reads the exhaust gases coming off the burner, including carbon monoxide and oxygen. We use it after install to confirm the furnace burns clean and safely. For you, it's proof the unit is tuned, not just running.

  • Digital manometer

    A manometer measures gas pressure at the valve and air pressure in the ducts. Wrong gas pressure hurts performance and safety. High duct pressure means the blower is straining, which causes noise and early wear. We take readings and fix what's off.

  • Gas leak detector

    Every new gas connection gets tested. An electronic sniffer picks up tiny leaks you'd never smell, and leak solution shows bubbles at a loose fitting. It takes a few minutes and it's the check I'd never skip.

Factors That Affect Gas Furnace Installation in Ottawa

No two furnace jobs look the same. A swap in a newer townhouse and a swap in a 1960s brick bungalow can be very different days. These are the things that change the work the most.

  • Winter cold and snow

    Ottawa can sit at minus 25 degrees Celsius for days. A furnace that's too small runs nonstop and still leaves the back bedroom cold. One that's too big short cycles, which wears out parts and wastes gas. We size from your home's heat loss. Side wall vents also need to sit well above snow drifts.

  • Age and style of the house

    Older homes in Nepean often have small ducts, tight furnace closets and chimneys built for a different kind of furnace. Newer homes in Kanata and Barrhaven usually have better ducts, but finished basements leave little room. We check the space before we pick a model.

  • Size and layout

    A two storey home with a long duct run to the top floor needs different airflow than a bungalow. Open basements make the work easy. Finished ceilings and tight corners make duct changes slow. A two stage furnace can even out the heat in a bigger layout.

  • Existing system and venting rules

    A mid efficiency furnace may vent into a chimney. A high efficiency one vents through a side wall with plastic pipe and needs a drain for condensate. In Ontario, gas work must be done by a certified gas technician under TSSA rules. Permit needs vary by job, and we'll tell you what applies to yours.

What to Consider Before Hiring a Gas Furnace Installation Professional

Anyone can put a furnace in a basement. Doing it safely, to code, and in a way that still works well in year ten is the harder part. Ask these questions before you sign anything, from us or from anyone else.

  • Gas certification: In Ontario, gas work must be done by a technician registered with the TSSA, so ask to see their certificate.
  • Insurance and licensing: Ask for proof of liability insurance and any business licence, and check that the paperwork is current.
  • Relevant experience: Ask how many furnaces like yours they've installed, especially if your home is older or has tricky ductwork.
  • Written estimates: Get the model, the scope of work, venting changes and the total price on paper, so you can compare quotes fairly.
  • Warranty terms: Ask what the manufacturer covers on parts, what the installer covers on labour, and for how long. Get it in writing.
  • Reviews and clarity: Read recent reviews, and notice whether they explain the job in words you understand or just throw out model numbers.

The cheapest quote often leaves things out, like permits, venting upgrades or a proper start up check. A good installer welcomes these questions. If you get vague answers or pressure to decide on the spot, keep looking.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Furnace Installation

These are the questions people ask us most before they book. If yours isn't here, call and we'll talk it through.

How long will I be without heat during the swap?

A straightforward replacement usually takes most of one working day. The old furnace comes out, the new one goes in, and we test it before we leave. If the vent pipe or ductwork has to change, it can run longer. We'll tell you what to expect when we look at the job, so you can plan around it.

Is it smarter to replace a furnace before an Ottawa winter than to wait for it to quit?

Usually, yes. A worn furnace tends to fail on the coldest night, and then you're picking a new one in a rush. Booking in late summer or fall gives you time to compare options and choose a day that suits you. Installers also get busy once the first hard cold arrives.

Can a high efficiency furnace go into an older house?

Most of the time, yes, but the venting changes. These units pull so much heat out of the exhaust that they vent through plastic pipe out a side wall, and they need a drain for condensate. If your old furnace used a masonry chimney, we'll check what to do with it.

What's the difference between a single stage and a two stage furnace?

A single stage furnace has one setting: full blast, then off. A two stage unit runs on low most of the time and switches to high only on the coldest days. That means fewer hot and cold swings, a quieter blower, and steadier heat from room to room. It's a good fit for larger or two storey homes.

How do you know what size furnace my house needs?

We don't just match the old unit. Many old furnaces were oversized to begin with, or the house has changed since with new windows, insulation or an addition. We look at the home's size, layout and heat loss. A furnace that's too big switches on and off constantly, which wears it out and leaves uneven rooms.

Will I need new ductwork or a new thermostat?

Often not. We check the ducts for leaks, crushed sections and a return that's too small, since those problems hurt any furnace. If they're sound, we leave them alone. A two stage furnace works best with a thermostat that can control both stages, so we'll tell you if yours won't do.

What should I have ready when I ask for a quote?

Take a photo of the label on your current furnace, and note roughly how old it is and the size of your home. Tell us what's been going wrong, like cold rooms, noise or high bills. We'll want to see the setup in person before giving a firm quote.
