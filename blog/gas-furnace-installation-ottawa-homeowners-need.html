Gas Furnace Installation in Ottawa: What to Know Before Winter Hits

Sizing, cost, timing and permits, explained plainly by an Ottawa heating company.

A furnace usually quits on the coldest night of January. That's a bad time to start shopping. Planning gas furnace installation in the fall gives you time to compare quotes, pick the right size, and skip a rushed decision.

Ottawa winters are hard on equipment. Lows near minus 25 are normal, and a furnace has to run for months. This guide covers the signs yours is near the end, how sizing works, what install day looks like, what it costs, and when to book. Ottawa Gas Furnace has done this work since 2019, so we'll tell you what we'd do in your basement.

Signs your furnace is on its way out

Age is the first clue. Most gas furnaces last 15 to 20 years. Many homes built in Kanata and Barrhaven in the early 2000s are reaching that point now. Older Nepean and Orleans houses often have a second or third furnace already.

Watch for these:

  • Uneven heat: some rooms stay cold while the furnace runs nonstop.
  • Rising bills: gas use climbs but the house feels the same.
  • Yellow or flickering flame: a healthy burner flame is steady and blue.
  • Short cycling: it starts and stops every few minutes.
  • Rust, soot or cracks: a cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide.

Our rule of thumb: if the unit is over 15 and a repair costs more than a third of a new furnace, replace it. A cracked heat exchanger is always a replacement. Keep working carbon monoxide alarms on every level, no matter how old the furnace is.

Sizing and efficiency: where most mistakes start

Bigger is not better. An oversized furnace heats the house fast, shuts off, and repeats. That wears out parts and leaves rooms uneven. Ask for a heat loss calculation based on your home's size, insulation and windows. Copying the old furnace's size just repeats an old guess.

Then pick the efficiency. A high efficiency furnace is rated around 96% AFUE or better, so almost all the gas you buy becomes heat. Older units may sit between 60 and 80%. Over an Ottawa winter, that gap shows up on the gas bill.

  • Single stage: full blast or off. Cheapest up front.
  • Two stage: runs low most days and high on the coldest ones. Quieter and more even.
  • Variable speed fan: moves air more gently and cuts hot and cold spots.

For most homes here, we lean toward two stage. The extra cost is modest, and you notice it in the bedrooms.

What gas furnace installation day looks like

Most jobs finish in one day, often five to eight hours. A venting change or ductwork repair can push it to two.

  • Shut off gas and power, then remove the old furnace.
  • Set the new unit and connect it to the ducts.
  • Run new venting. A high efficiency furnace uses plastic pipe out a side wall, not the chimney.
  • Connect the condensate drain, since these furnaces make water.
  • Reconnect gas, test for leaks, and check the burner and airflow.
  • Show you the thermostat and how to change the filter.

Pay attention to the condensate line. If the drain runs through a cold corner of the basement, it can freeze in January. A good installer plans for that.

Also ask about the old chimney. Once the furnace is off it, the flue may need a liner or a cap, especially if the water heater still vents there.

What it costs and what moves the price

In Ottawa, a replacement often lands between $4,500 and $8,000 installed. Treat that as a ballpark, not a quote. Your real number depends on your house.

These things move it most:

  • Furnace size and efficiency level
  • Single stage, two stage, or variable speed
  • New venting or a chimney liner
  • Ductwork fixes, like a leaky or undersized return
  • A new thermostat or air cleaner
  • How hard it is to get the old unit out

Get at least two written quotes that name the brand, model and efficiency rating. If one is far lower, find out what's missing. It's often the venting, the permit, or the cleanup.

Ask about rebates too. Enbridge and federal programs have offered incentives for efficient equipment. They change often, so confirm before you sign.

Permits, timing and choosing an installer

Gas work in Ontario falls under the TSSA, and the installer normally pulls the gas permit. Ask to see it. Venting or ductwork changes can bring in the City of Ottawa too, so ask who handles each piece before you sign.

Book in September or October if you can. Once the first hard cold snap hits, schedules fill and you lose the chance to compare. Late spring works well too.

When you talk to installers, ask:

  • Will you do a heat loss calculation, or just match the old size?
  • Is the permit included in the price?
  • What exact brand, model and efficiency rating are you quoting?
  • Who removes the old unit and caps the old vent?
  • Who do I call if something acts up in week two?

A clear answer matters more than a low number. If they dodge, move on.
